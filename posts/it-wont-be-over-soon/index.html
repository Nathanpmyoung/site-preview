It won't be over soon

Optimism and imagination

2025-07-22 · first published on Substack

Hey, what can you say? We were overdue
But it'll be over soon, you wait

Growing up I recall being scared of Heaven.

I lay there in my bunk bed, just imagining that time went on and on and would never nearly be over.

I don't think this was fear of bad things, either. I recall worrying about endless games of Monopoly, which I enjoyed. I think it was the unendingness that was the main concern.

I see this mirrored in media.

But I'm not sure it will be over soon. I think, almost certainly, it'll continue for a long time yet.

I think our civilisation is going to continue. I don't think human civilisation is going to be destroyed by AI. And I don't think the US is going to be destroyed by China. Though in both cases, things might get weirder than people expect and there are opportunities for bad outcomes.

I think our lives might continue for a long time yet. That there is a real chance—not high, but real—that we live for functionally forever. I am very confident our brains are physical things that decay according to physical processes. If we can halt that decay, we can live arbitrarily long times. Again, I’m not saying this will be perfect, it will come with its own set of problems.

We are half-hearted creatures, fooling about with drink and sex and ambition when infinite joy is offered us, like an ignorant child who wants to go on making mud pies in a slum because he cannot imagine what is meant by the offer of a holiday at the sea.

C. S. Lewis

For me, I think my fear was a failure of trust, imagination, and responsibility.

I didn’t fully trust that God would have Heaven in hand. Many people have similarly lost trust in capitalism or a US-led world order. We have, in some larger sense, become atheists.

I think this is misguided1. We have a reasonable track record of improving things. I trust the human race to further improve. This seems a strange time to give up.

Next, I couldn’t imagine an enjoyable life of arbitrary length. Somehow the distance stretched away from me like looking down one of the new Elizabeth Line trains. Vertiginous.

I think many struggle to imagine a good world that branches off this one. Bo Burnham seems depressed. And it seems bad to have such a wealthy, successful cultural icon mired in depression. Many such cases.

These days, I think I can imagine Heaven being good for a long time. There are many books to read, many games to play, many problems to solve, many new people to be. And if after all of that fails, I imagine Heaven contains suicide too. But just not for a while.

https://nathanpmyoung.substack.com/p/the-good-place-all-good-things-must-come-to-an-end-5fe5c641173f

I am wary that my views of the future are in fact a diagnosis of my mental state. I think in reality the future and my mental wellbeing are not closely correlated. I worry that a crop of unhappy artists is convincing us that everyone is unhappy. That our problems are unfixable. So we shouldn’t try. I don’t think that’s true.

Finally, it was a failure of responsibility. Several of my friends have at times been suicidal. And they have discussed being surprised to still be alive, figuring that they would end their lives at 20 or 30.

As a child, I think I struggled to really imagine being stable and happy. Doing work I liked. Being married. And so I didn’t try to figure out what was wrong. Only in my mid 20s did I make big strides to get a life that I actually enjoyed.

On some level, I think that Western people are stuck in a similar malaise. They cannot imagine a world better than this one so they would rather imagine that it blows up somehow, that rather than having to fix their mistakes step by step2.

But I think things will go on regardless, in which case, why not fix some of the issues we dislike—try and allow more people to live safe, happy lives, find a way to solve conflict between China and the US as we did between Europe’s great powers, fall in love and get married.

Hate it or loathe it, I don’t think we can get off this ride any time soon. The future will likely stretch out from the present and we will have new problems and new opportunities. There is some chance that AI kills us (far higher than I’d like) but I wouldn’t bank on it3.

I think there is a small chance that you will live as long as you wish. And good odds that the transhuman race will muddle on for millenia in some form or other. And yes, that scares me. But it thrills me too.

It won’t be over soon.

So put on your trousers, one leg at a time, and face the world.
